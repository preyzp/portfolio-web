## Overview

Kemang Village Residences is a real residential development, and Kemvill is its resident companion app by IFCA — one place to pay bills, book facilities, raise requests, file goods permits, track utilities, follow community events, and read house rules. The project was a full mobile redesign of that experience: rebuilding the interface module by module into one consistent, modern system, delivered in both English and Indonesian.

The audience is residents of Kemang Village. A single person uses the app for very different things across a month — paying an invoice, booking a court, reporting a leak, registering a delivery — so the goal was to make all of it feel like one trustworthy app rather than a dozen separate tools.

## My Role & Responsibilities

I was the mobile UI/UX designer on this project. Working from the product requirements and IFCA's existing design system, I designed the resident experience end to end: I structured the information architecture across twelve modules, defined interaction patterns and screen states, built the UI and layouts in Figma, adapted and reused components from the IFCA system, and established a shared status and navigation language. I also produced the full English localization alongside the Indonesian screens and prepared the designs for developer handoff.

The team owned the product scope and business rules, the backend, data and integrations, roles and permissions, and the underlying platform and release.

## The Challenge

Before the redesign, residents juggled separate flows — and calls to management — for bills, bookings, complaints and permits. Scattered tasks mean missed payments and a heavier support load. The deeper problem was consistency: one resident does very different things and shouldn't have to learn a new pattern each time.

The design goal was to unify many modules under one visual language, navigation and status system, so every task feels like the same, trustworthy app.

> How might we bring every resident task — paying, booking, requesting, tracking — into a single mobile app that feels consistent, calm and easy to trust?

## Design Process

I started with information architecture: a single dashboard that routes to every resident service, with each module owning its own list, detail and action flows while sharing navigation, status language and components. From there I designed screen structure and visual hierarchy, interaction patterns and screen states, then the UI itself in Figma — adapting components from IFCA's system rather than reinventing them.

Four key UX decisions shaped the app:

1. **Making money legible** — Bills were split into Invoices and Receipts, each with unmistakable Paid / Unpaid states, due dates and honest empty states, so payment status reads at a glance.
2. **One status language everywhere** — a single set of status chips and colours (Paid, Unpaid, In Progress, Completed, Waiting) reused across every module, so status means the same thing throughout the product.
3. **Utilities you can actually read** — each utility leads with a bill hero and a six-month usage chart, then itemises meters and charges below for anyone who wants the detail.
4. **Booking without friction** — a clear browse → detail → schedule → confirm flow, with capacity, hours and house rules surfaced before booking.

![Facility booking flow — open the app, browse facilities, review and book](/images/portfolio/kemang-village-residences/03-process.webp)

## Final Solution

The result is a single, coherent app that carries a resident across twelve modules — home dashboard, bills and payments, facility booking, requests and complaints, goods and work permits, concierge and packages, utilities, announcements, community events, house rules, and account and unit management — without changing patterns between them. Money is clearer, dense utility data is made glanceable, and booking is guided step by step. Because the modules share one component set and one status language, a broad super-app still feels like one product.

![Key screens — legible bills, one status language, readable utilities, frictionless booking](/images/portfolio/kemang-village-residences/04-solution.webp)

## Platform & Interface Showcase

Every screen ships in two languages. Text and components were structured so labels swap between Indonesian and English without breaking layout — dates, currency, statuses and longer strings all accounted for, so longer English text reflows without clipping. The app keeps Kemang Village's garden-residence character throughout: an olive-green brand, soft surfaces and rounded cards, with colour carrying status while the layout stays quiet.

![The same home screen in English and Indonesian, showing full bilingual parity](/images/portfolio/kemang-village-residences/05-responsive.webp)

## Outcomes & Learnings

This was a qualitative project, so the outcomes are about clarity, consistency and trust rather than measured results: a single, coherent app for every resident task; clearer money, with invoices, receipts and statuses readable at a glance; one status language reused across all twelve modules; dense data such as utilities and permits made readable on mobile; reusable components that speed up implementation; and full English and Indonesian parity across the app.

The hard part wasn't any single screen — it was holding a dozen different services together so they feel like one calm app. Designing within IFCA's system reinforced that reuse and a shared status language aren't constraints; they're what let a broad product stay coherent as it grows, and the same discipline is what keeps two languages in sync.
