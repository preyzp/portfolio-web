## Overview

The Bellezza is a residential development, and this is the resident companion app I designed for it — one place to pay bills, book facilities, raise requests and complaints, file goods permits, track package deliveries through concierge, read house rules, and follow community events. The experience ships in both English and Indonesian.

The audience is residents. A single person uses the app for very different things — settling an invoice, booking a court, reporting a repair, registering a delivery — so the goal was to make all of it feel like one consistent, trustworthy app.

![The Bellezza resident app — home, bills, requests and community screens](/images/portfolio/the-bellezza/02-overview.webp)

## My Role & Responsibilities

I was the mobile UI/UX designer. I structured the information architecture across the app's modules, defined the interaction patterns and screen states, designed the UI and layouts in Figma, and built a shared status and navigation language so every module feels part of the same product. I also produced the app in two languages, English and Indonesian, and prepared the screens for developer handoff.

The product scope and business rules, the backend and integrations, roles and permissions, and the release were owned by the wider team.

## The Challenge

Resident services are easy to scatter: bills in one place, bookings in another, complaints over the phone, permits on paper. Spread across separate flows, everyday tasks get missed and the management office carries a heavier load.

The design goal was to bring every resident task into a single app with one visual language, one navigation model and one status system — and to do it in two languages without the layout breaking.

> How might we bring paying, booking, requesting and tracking into one resident app that stays calm and consistent, in both English and Indonesian?

## Design Process

I organized the app around a home dashboard that routes to each service, with every module following the same list, detail and action pattern. A few decisions shaped the work:

1. **Clear money** — bills are split into invoices and receipts, each with unmistakable paid / unpaid states, due dates and honest empty states.
2. **One status language** — a shared set of status chips and colours (paid, unpaid, in progress, completed, cancelled) reused across bills, requests, permits and bookings.
3. **Guided requests and bookings** — creating a request or booking a facility follows a short, predictable flow, with capacity, hours and house rules surfaced before the resident commits.
4. **A guest mode** — a scoped, read-limited experience so visitors can see what they need without a full resident account.

![Requests flow — open requests, create a request, confirmation](/images/portfolio/the-bellezza/03-process.webp)

## Final Solution

The result is a single app that carries a resident across bills and payments, facility booking, requests and complaints, goods permits, concierge and package handling, utilities, house rules and community events — each built from the same components and status language. Supporting pieces like a guest mode and a one-tap emergency call round out the day-to-day experience.

![Key modules — bills, facility booking, goods permits, concierge and packages](/images/portfolio/the-bellezza/04-solution.webp)

## Platform & Interface Showcase

Every screen ships in two languages. Text and components were structured so labels swap between Indonesian and English without breaking layout — dates, currency, statuses and longer strings all accounted for, so the two versions stay in parity.

![The same home screen in English and Indonesian, showing full bilingual parity](/images/portfolio/the-bellezza/05-responsive.webp)

## Outcomes & Learnings

This was a qualitative project, so the outcomes are about clarity, consistency and trust rather than measured results: a single, coherent app for every resident task; clearer money through separated invoices and receipts; one status language reused across modules; guided flows for requests and bookings; and full English and Indonesian parity.

The lesson echoed what I value most in product work — that a shared shell and a consistent status language aren't limits; they're what let a broad set of services stay simple as it grows, and the discipline that keeps one product coherent is the same discipline that keeps two languages in sync.
